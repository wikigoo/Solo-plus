**پروژه استارگیت** یک واحد مخفی ارتش ایالات متحده بود که در سال 1977 [\ [^1] [^2] فورت [مید](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Meade "فورت مید") ، [مریلند](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "مریلند") ، توسط [آژانس اطلاعات دفاعی](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency "آژانس اطلاعات دفاعی") (DIA) و [موسسه تحقیقاتی استنفورد](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRI_International "موسسه بین‌المللی SRI") (SRI)، یک پیمانکار کالیفرنیایی، برای بررسی پتانسیل پدیده‌های [روانی](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychic "روانی") در کاربردهای نظامی و اطلاعات داخلی تأسیس شد. این پروژه و پروژه‌های پیشین و خواهرخوانده آن، در ابتدا با [نام‌های رمز](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_name "نام کد") مختلفی - از جمله "Gondola Wish"، "Grill Flame" و "Center Lane" تحت INSCOM، "Sun Streak" تحت DIA [^3] و "Star Gate" تحت [فرماندهی اطلاعات خارجی ارتش ایالات متحده](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Foreign_Intelligence_Command "فرماندهی اطلاعات خارجی ارتش ایالات متحده") (USAFIC) در سال 1993، [^4] "Star Gate" و "SCANATE" تحت [آژانس اطلاعات مرکزی](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency "سازمان اطلاعات مرکزی") (CIA) و "Project CF" [^5] \- تا سال 1991، زمانی که آنها ادغام شدند و به طور جمعی به "پروژه استارگیت" تغییر نام دادند، شناخته می‌شدند.

کار پروژه استارگیت در درجه اول شامل [مشاهده از راه دور](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_viewing "مشاهده از راه دور") بود ، توانایی ادعایی برای "دیدن" روانی رویدادها، مکان‌ها یا [اطلاعات](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information "اطلاعات") از فاصله بسیار دور. [^6] این پروژه تا سال 1987 توسط ستوان فردریک هولمز "اسکیپ" اتواتر (متولد 1947 [^7] )، دستیار و "جستجوگر ذهنی" سرلشکر [آلبرت استابلبین](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Stubblebine "Albert Stubblebine") ، و بعداً رئیس [موسسه مونرو،](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Monroe#The_Monroe_Institute "Robert Monroe") نظارت می‌شد. [^8] [^8] [^8] این واحد در مقیاس کوچک بود و شامل حدود 15 تا 20 نفر می‌شد و از "یک سربازخانه چوبی قدیمی و نشت‌دار" اداره می‌شد. [^9] [^9] [^9]

پروژه استارگیت در سال ۱۹۹۵ پس از آنکه یک بررسی سفارش داده شده توسط سازمان سیا به این نتیجه رسید که هرگز در هیچ عملیات اطلاعاتی مفید نبوده است، خاتمه یافت و از طبقه‌بندی خارج شد. اگرچه اثرات آماری معنی‌داری در آزمایش‌های آزمایشگاهی مشاهده شد، اما بررسی‌کنندگان مطمئن نبودند که آیا این نتیجه خطاها بوده است یا خیر، و اطلاعات ارائه شده توسط این برنامه مبهم و شامل داده‌های نامربوط و اشتباه بود. [^10]<sup><span title="صفحه / مکان: ۵–۴"><font dir="auto"><font dir="auto">:</font></font>  <font dir="auto"><font dir="auto">5–4</font></font> </span></sup> این برنامه در [کتاب سال ۲۰۰۴](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Men_Who_Stare_at_Goats "مردانی که به بزها خیره می‌شوند") و [فیلم سال ۲۰۰۹](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Men_Who_Stare_at_Goats_\(film\) "مردانی که به بزها خیره می‌شوند (فیلم)") *«مردانی که به بزها خیره می‌شوند* [^11] [^12] [^13] [^14] به نمایش گذاشته شد ، اگرچه هیچ‌کدام از آنها نامی از آن نبردند.

## پیشینه

به گفته [جوزف مک‌مونیگل](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McMoneagle "جوزف مک‌مونگل") ، سازمان سیا و آژانس اطلاعات دفاعی آمریکا (DIA) در واکنش به گزارش‌هایی مبنی بر اینکه شوروی‌ها به طور فعال در حال تحقیق در زمینه فراروانشناسی هستند، برنامه‌های تحقیقاتی خود را تأیید و تأمین مالی کردند. مک‌مونیگل نوشت که بررسی این برنامه‌ها هر شش ماه یکبار در سطح [کمیته منتخب](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Select_or_special_committee_\(United_States_Congress\) "کمیته منتخب یا ویژه (کنگره ایالات متحده)") سنا و مجلس نمایندگان انجام می‌شود. به گفته مک‌مونیگل، رویه عملیاتی استاندارد برای مشاهده از راه دور این بود که نتایج از "بیننده" مخفی نگه داشته شود تا شکست‌ها به اعتماد و مهارت بیننده آسیب نرساند. [^15]

مک‌مونگل، مشاهده از راه دور را به عنوان تلاشی برای حس کردن اطلاعات ناشناخته در مورد مکان‌ها یا رویدادها تعریف می‌کند و می‌گوید که این کار معمولاً برای تشخیص رویدادهای جاری انجام می‌شود، اما در طول برنامه‌های اطلاعاتی نظامی و داخلی، بینندگان ادعا می‌کردند که چیزهایی را در آینده حس می‌کنند و [پیشگویی را](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precognition "پیش آگاهی") تجربه می‌کنند. [^16]

## تاریخچه

### دهه ۱۹۷۰

در سال 1970، منابع اطلاعاتی ایالات متحده معتقد بودند که اتحاد جماهیر شوروی سالانه 60 میلیون [روبل](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_ruble#Sixth_Soviet_ruble,_1961_-_1991 "روبل شوروی") برای تحقیقات " [روانگردان](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_harassment "آزار و اذیت الکترونیکی") " هزینه می‌کند. در پاسخ به ادعاهایی مبنی بر اینکه برنامه شوروی نتایجی به همراه داشته است، سازمان سیا در همان سال بودجه‌ای را برای برنامه جدیدی به نام SCANATE ("اسکن با مختصات") آغاز کرد. [^17] تحقیقات مشاهده از راه دور در سال 1972 در [موسسه تحقیقاتی استنفورد](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRI_International "موسسه بین‌المللی SRI") (SRI) در [منلو پارک، کالیفرنیا](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menlo_Park,_California "منلو پارک، کالیفرنیا") آغاز شد. [^17] [^18] طرفداران این تحقیق ( [راسل تارگ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Targ "راسل تارگ") و [هارولد ای. پوتهوف](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_E._Puthoff "هارولد ای. پوتهاف") ) گفتند که حداقل میزان دقت 65٪ مورد نیاز مراجعین، اغلب در آزمایش‌های بعدی فراتر رفته است. [^17]

[فیزیکدانان](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist "فیزیکدان") تارگ و پوتهوف در سال 1972 آزمایش افراد دارای قدرت‌های فراحسی را برای SRI آغاز کردند، از جمله [یوری گلر](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Geller "یوری گلر") اسرائیلی که بعدها به یک چهره مشهور بین‌المللی تبدیل شد. نتایج ظاهراً موفقیت‌آمیز آنها توجه [وزارت دفاع ایالات متحده](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "وزارت دفاع ایالات متحده") را به خود جلب کرد. سرهنگ دوم آستین دبلیو کیبلر (1930-2008) \- که در آن زمان مدیر تحقیقات رفتاری برای [ARPA بود - از](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA "دارپا") [ری هایمن](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Hyman "ری هایمن") ، استاد روانشناسی در [دانشگاه اورگان](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon "دانشگاه اورگان") ، خواست تا به SRI برود و در مورد او تحقیق کند. او قرار بود به طور خاص گلر را ارزیابی کند. گزارش هایمن به دولت این بود که گلر یک "کلاهبردار کامل" است و در نتیجه تارگ و پوتهوف قرارداد دولتی خود را برای همکاری بیشتر با او از دست دادند. نتیجه، یک تور تبلیغاتی برای گلر، تارگ و پوتهوف بود تا به دنبال بودجه خصوصی برای تحقیقات بیشتر روی گلر باشند. [^19] [^19] [^19]

یکی از موفقیت‌های این پروژه، یافتن محل یک هواپیمای جاسوسی گمشده شوروی در سال 1976 توسط رزماری اسمیت، دستیار اداری جوانی که توسط مدیر پروژه، دیل گراف، استخدام شده بود، بود. [^20]

در سال 1977، واحد بهره‌برداری از سیستم‌های دستیار رئیس ستاد ارتش (ACSI) برنامه Gondola Wish را برای "ارزیابی کاربردهای بالقوه دشمن از مشاهده از راه دور" آغاز کرد. [^17] سپس اطلاعات ارتش این برنامه را در اواسط سال 1978 به عنوان یک برنامه عملیاتی Grill Flame، مستقر در ساختمان‌های 2560 و 2561 در [فورت مید](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Meade "فورت مید") در مریلند ( "واحد G") [INSCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Intelligence_and_Security_Command "فرماندهی اطلاعات و امنیت ارتش ایالات متحده") ، رسمیت بخشید. [^17]

### دهه ۱۹۸۰

در اوایل سال 1979، تحقیقات در SRI در "Grill Flame" ادغام شد که در سال 1983 به پروژه "Center Lane" (ICLP) INSCOM تغییر نام داد. در سال 1984، وجود این برنامه توسط [جک اندرسون](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Anderson_\(columnist\) "جک اندرسون (ستون‌نویس)") گزارش شد و در آن سال توسط [شورای تحقیقات ملی](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academies_of_Sciences,_Engineering,_and_Medicine "آکادمی‌های ملی علوم، مهندسی و پزشکی") [آکادمی ملی علوم](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences "آکادمی ملی علوم") با واکنش نامطلوبی مواجه شد. در اواخر سال 1985، بودجه ارتش قطع شد، اما این برنامه با نام "Sun Streak" تغییر نام داد و توسط اداره اطلاعات علمی و فنی DIA (کد دفتر DT-S) تأمین مالی شد. [^17] [^17] [^17]

[George Stephanopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stephanopoulos "جورج استفانوپولوس"), in his 2024 book *The Situation Room*, mentions the project in discussing a May 8, 1980, Situation Room briefing for [President Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "جیمی کارتر"), after Carter's failed [hostage rescue mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis "بحران گروگانگیری ایران") in Iran on April 24, 1980.[^21] In a 2005 [GQ magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ "جی کیو") interview, Carter said CIA director [Stansfield Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stansfield_Turner "استنسفیلد ترنر") told him the agency once contacted a California woman who claimed to have psychic powers to help locate a missing plane.[^22] [^23]

### 1990s

In 1991, most of the contracting for the program was transferred from SRI to [Science Applications International Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Applications_International_Corporation "شرکت بین‌المللی کاربردهای علمی") (SAIC), with Edwin May controlling 70% of the contractor funds and 85% of the data. Its security classification was altered from [Special Access Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_access_program "برنامه دسترسی ویژه") (SAP) to [Limited Dissemination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classified_information#Secret "اطلاعات طبقه‌بندی‌شده") (LIMDIS), and it was given its final name, STARGATE.[^17]

### Closure (1995)

In 1995, the defense appropriations bill directed that the program be transferred from DIA to CIA oversight. The CIA commissioned a report by the [American Institutes for Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institutes_for_Research "موسسات تحقیقاتی آمریکا") (AIR) that found that remote viewing had not been proved to work by a psychic mechanism, and said it had not been used operationally.[^10]<sup><span title="صفحه / مکان: ۵–۴">: 5–4</span> </sup> The CIA subsequently cancelled and declassified the program.[^17]

In 1995 the project was transferred to the CIA and a retrospective evaluation of the results was done. The appointed panel consisted primarily of [Jessica Utts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Utts "جسیکا آتس"), [Meena Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meena_Shah "مینا شاه") and [Ray Hyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Hyman "ری هایمن"). Utts and Hyman were appointed because, in addition to their extensive scientific credentials, they represented both sides of the paranormal controversy, although the AIR considered both of them 'fair and open-minded scientists'.[^10] Hyman had produced an unflattering report on Uri Geller and SRI for the government two decades earlier, but the psychologist [David Marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Marks_\(psychologist\) "دیوید مارکس (روانشناس)") found Utts' appointment to the review panel "puzzling" given that she had published papers with Edwin May, one of the main researchers on the Stargate Project, considering this joint research likely to make her "less than \[im\]partial".[^6]

A report by Utts claimed the results were evidence of psychic functioning; however, Hyman in his report argued Utts's conclusion that ESP had been proven to exist, especially precognition, was premature and the findings had not been independently replicated.[^24] Hyman came to the conclusion:

> Psychologists, such as myself, who study subjective validation find nothing striking or surprising in the reported matching of reports against targets in the Stargate data. The overwhelming amount of data generated by the viewers is vague, general, and way off target. The few apparent hits are just what we would expect if nothing other than reasonable guessing and subjective validation are operating.[^25]

The review concluded:

> The foregoing observations provide a compelling argument against continuation of the program within the intelligence community. Even though a statistically significant effect has been observed in the laboratory, it remains unclear whether the existence of a paranormal phenomenon, remote viewing, has been demonstrated. The laboratory studies do not provide evidence regarding the origins or nature of the phenomenon, assuming it exists, nor do they address an important methodological issue of inter-judge reliability.
> 
> Further, even if it could be demonstrated unequivocally that a paranormal phenomenon occurs under the conditions present in the laboratory paradigm, these conditions have limited applicability and utility for intelligence gathering operations. For example, the nature of the remote viewing targets are vastly dissimilar, as are the specific tasks required of the remote viewers. Most importantly, the information provided by remote viewing is vague and ambiguous, making it difficult, if not impossible, for the technique to yield information of sufficient quality and accuracy of information for actionable intelligence. Thus, we conclude that continued use of remote viewing in intelligence gathering operations is not warranted.[^10]<sup><span title="صفحه / مکان: E-4–E-5">: E-4–E-5</span></sup>

[Joe Nickell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Nickell "جو نیکل") wrote:

> Other evaluators — two psychologists from AIR — assessed the potential intelligence-gathering usefulness of remote viewing. They concluded that the alleged psychic technique was of dubious value and lacked the concreteness and reliability necessary for it to be used as a basis for making decisions or taking action. The final report found "reason to suspect" that in "some well publicised cases of dramatic hits" the remote viewers might have had "substantially more background information" than might otherwise be apparent.[^26]

According to the AIR review, no remote viewing report ever provided actionable information for any intelligence operation.[^27] [^10]<sup><span title="صفحه / مکان: ۵–۴">: 5–4</span></sup>

Based upon the collected findings, which recommended a higher level of critical research and tighter controls, the CIA terminated the 20 million dollar project, citing a lack of documented evidence that the program had any value to the intelligence community. *Time* magazine stated in 1995 three full-time psychics were still working on a $500,000-a-year budget out of [Fort Meade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Meade "فورت مید"), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland "مریلند"), which would soon close.[^27]

David Marks in his book *[The Psychology of the Psychic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Psychology_of_the_Psychic "روانشناسی روان")* (2000) discussed the flaws in the Stargate Project in detail.[^6] Marks wrote that there were six negative design features of the experiments. The possibility of cues or [sensory leakage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory_leakage "نشت حسی") was not ruled out, no [independent replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproducibility "تکرارپذیری"), some experiments were conducted in secret, making peer-review impossible. Marks noted that the judge Edwin May was also the principal investigator for the project and this was problematic, making a huge conflict of interest with collusion, cuing and fraud being possible. Marks concluded the project was nothing more than a "subjective delusion" and after two decades of research it had failed to provide any scientific evidence for the legitimacy of remote viewing.[^6]

Some members of the project expressed surprise at these findings, such as Dale Graff, who recalled his bafflement at the announcement that the project was a failure when in his experience as one of the investigators it had seemed to him to be producing definite successes. He commented that it was not surprising that no information from the project had ever resulted in military or intelligence action being taken, as the military and intelligence authorities had never really taken the project seriously and were reluctant to take action based only on Stargate data.[^28]

In January 2017, the CIA published records online of the Stargate Project as part of the CREST archive.[^29]

## Methodology

According to Joseph McMoneagle, the Stargate Project created a set of protocols designed to make the research of clairvoyance and [out-of-body experiences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-body_experience "تجربه خروج از بدن") more [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method "روش علمی"), and to minimize as much as possible session noise and inaccuracy. He wrote that the term "remote viewing" emerged as shorthand to describe this more structured approach to clairvoyance. McMoneagle said Project Stargate would only receive a mission after all other intelligence attempts, methods, or approaches had already been exhausted.[^16]<sup><span title="صفحه / مکان: ۲۱">: 21</span></sup>

McMoneagle claims that at peak manpower there were over 22 active military and civilian remote viewers providing data, and people leaving the project were not replaced so that when the project closed in 1995 this number had dwindled down to three, one of whom was using [tarot cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarot "تاروت"). According to McMoneagle, "The Army never had a truly open attitude toward psychic functioning", hence, the use of the term "giggle factor".[^30] and the saying, "I wouldn't want to be found dead next to a psychic".[^15]

## Civilian personnel

### Harold E. Puthoff

In the 1970s, CIA and DIA granted funds to Harold E. Puthoff to investigate [paranormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranormal "ماوراءالطبیعه") abilities, collaborating with [Russell Targ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Targ "راسل تارگ") in a study of the purported [psychic abilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_psychic_abilities "فهرست توانایی‌های روانی") of [Uri Geller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Geller "یوری گلر"), [Ingo Swann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingo_Swann "اینگو سوان"), [Pat Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Price_\(remote_viewer\) "پت پرایس (بیننده از راه دور)"), [Joseph McMoneagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McMoneagle "جوزف مک‌مونگل"), and others as part of the Stargate Project,[^31] of which Puthoff became a director.[^32]

As with Ingo Swann and Pat Price, Puthoff attributed much of his personal remote viewing skills to his involvement with [Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientology "ساینتولوژی") whereby he had attained, at that time, the highest level. All three eventually left Scientology in the late 1970s.

Puthoff worked as the principal investigator of the project. His team of psychics is said to have identified spies, located Soviet weapons and technologies, such as a nuclear submarine in 1979 and helped find lost [SCUD missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCUD_missile "موشک اسکاد") in the first [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War "جنگ خلیج فارس") and plutonium in [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea "کره شمالی") in 1994.[^33]

### Russell Targ

![](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Russell_Targ,_physicist.jpg)

Russell Targ

In the 1970s, Russell Targ began working with Harold Puthoff on the Stargate Project, while working with him as a researcher at Stanford Research Institute.[^34] [^35]

### Edwin May

Edwin C. May joined the Stargate Project in 1975 as a consultant and was working full-time in 1976. The original project was part of the Cognitive Sciences Laboratory managed by May. With more funding in 1991 May took the project to the Palo Alto offices at SAIC. This would last until 1995 when the CIA closed the project.[^6]

May worked as the principal investigator, judge and the star gatekeeper for the project. Marks says this was a serious weakness for the experiments as May had conflict of interest and could have done whatever he wanted with the data. Marks has written that May refused to release the names of the "oversight committee" and refused permission for him to give an independent judging of the Stargate transcripts. Marks found this suspicious, commenting "this refusal suggests that something must be wrong with the data or with the methods of data selection." [^6]

### Ingo Swann

Originally tested in the "Phase One" were OOBE-Beacon "RV" experiments at the [American Society for Psychical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Psychical_Research "انجمن آمریکایی تحقیقات روانشناختی"),[^36] under research director [Karlis Osis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlis_Osis "کارلیس اوسیس"). A former [OT VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_Thetan "عامل تتان") Scientologist,[^37] who alleged to have coined the term 'remote viewing' as a derivation of protocols originally developed by [René Warcollier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Warcollier "رنه وارکولیه"), a French chemical engineer in the early 20th century, documented in his book.[^38] Swann's achievement was to break free from the conventional mold of casual experimentation and candidate burn out, and develop a viable set of protocols that put clairvoyance within a framework named "Coordinate Remote Viewing" (CRV).[^39] In a 1995 letter Edwin C. May wrote he had not used Swann for two years, because there were rumors of him briefing a high-level person at [Science Applications International Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Applications_International_Corporation "شرکت بین‌المللی کاربردهای علمی") (SAIC) and the CIA on remote viewing, aliens, and ETs.[^40]

### Pat Price

A former Burbank, California, police officer and former Scientologist who participated in a number of [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "جنگ سرد") era remote viewing experiments, including the U.S. government-sponsored projects SCANATE and the Stargate Project. Price joined the program after a chance encounter with fellow Scientologists (at the time) Harold Puthoff and Ingo Swann near SRI.[^41] Working with maps and photographs provided to him by the CIA, Price claimed to have been able to retrieve information from facilities behind Soviet lines. He is probably best known for his sketches of cranes and gantries which appeared to conform to CIA intelligence photographs. At the time, the CIA took his claims seriously.[^42]

## Military personnel

### Lieutenant General James Clapper

The project leader in the 1990s was Lt. Gen. Clapper who later would serve as the Director of National Intelligence.[^43]

### Major General Albert Stubblebine

![](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Albert_Stubblebine_(US_Army_major_general).jpg)

Albert Stubblebine

A key sponsor of the research internally at [Fort Meade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Meade "فورت مید"), Maryland, Maj. Gen. Stubblebine was convinced of the reality of a wide variety of psychic phenomena. He required that all of his battalion commanders learn how to bend spoons *à la* [Uri Geller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Geller "یوری گلر"), and he himself attempted several psychic feats, even attempting to walk through walls. In the early 1980s he was responsible for the [United States Army Intelligence and Security Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Intelligence_and_Security_Command "فرماندهی اطلاعات و امنیت ارتش ایالات متحده") (INSCOM), during which time the remote viewing project in the US Army began. Some commentators have confused a "Project Jedi", allegedly run by [Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces "نیروهای ویژه ارتش ایالات متحده") primarily out of [Fort Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bragg "فورت براگ"), with Stargate. After some controversy involving these experiments, including alleged security violations from uncleared civilian psychics working in Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities (SCIFs), Stubblebine was placed on retirement. His successor as the INSCOM commander was Maj. Gen. [Harry E. Soyster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_E._Soyster "هری ای. سویستر"), who had a reputation as a much more conservative and conventional intelligence officer. Soyster was not amenable to continuing paranormal experiments and the Army's participation in Project Stargate ended during his tenure.[^15]

### David Morehouse

In his book, *[Psychic Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychic_Warrior_\(book\) "Psychic Warrior (book)")*, Morehouse claims to have worked on hundreds of remote viewing assignments, from searching for a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Armed_Forces "Soviet Armed Forces") jet that crashed in the jungle carrying an [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_nuclear_incident_terminology#Broken_Arrow "United States military nuclear incident terminology"), to tracking suspected [double agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_agent "Double agent").[^44]

### Joseph McMoneagle

McMoneagle claims he had a remarkable memory of very early childhood events. He grew up surrounded by alcoholism, abuse and poverty. As a child, he had visions at night when scared, and began to hone his psychic abilities in his teens for his own protection when he hitchhiked. He enlisted in the Army to get away from home. McMoneagle became an experimental remote viewer while serving in U.S. Army Intelligence.[^15]

### Ed Dames

Ed Dames' role was intended to be as session monitor and analyst as an aid to Fred Atwater [^45] rather than a remote viewer, Dames received no formal remote viewing training. After his assignment to the remote viewing unit at the end of January 1986, he was used to "run" remote viewers (as monitor) and provide training and practice sessions to viewer personnel. He soon established a reputation for pushing CRV to extremes, with target sessions on [Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis "Atlantis"), [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars "Mars"), [UFOs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO "UFO"), and aliens. He has been a frequent guest on the *[Coast to Coast AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_to_Coast_AM "Coast to Coast AM")* radio shows.[^46]

## Archives of the Impossible

The [Archives of the Impossible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_of_the_Impossible "Archives of the Impossible") (AOTI) of [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University "Rice University") at [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_Texas "Houston, Texas") is a [special collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_collection "Special collection") founded in 2014 by [Jeffrey J. Kripal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_J._Kripal "Jeffrey J. Kripal"), a [professor of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_studies "Religious studies").[^47] [^48] AOTI is based at the [Woodson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodson_Research_Center "Woodson Research Center") (WRC) and materials are housed in the [Fondren Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fondren_Library "Fondren Library").[^54] [^55] AOTI holds declassified research material of the Stargate Project.[^55] [^56] Christopher Senn was identified as organizing the Stargate Project collection for Rice University.[^57]<sup><span title="Page / location: 129">: 129</span> </sup> The collection was donated by Edwin May, the U.S. Army's program director from 1985 to 1995.[^55] [^56] In [Routledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Routledge "Routledge") 's *Handbook of Religion and Secrecy* by [Hugh Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Urban "Hugh Urban") and Paul Christopher Johnson, Kripal and Senn detailed that May's donation to AOTI encompassed thousands of pages of declassified materials.[^57]<sup><span title="Page / location: 129">: 129</span> </sup> Editor Derek Askey of *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_\(magazine\) "The Sun (magazine)")* in 2025 detailed his visit to AOTI, and his examination of materials from the government's Stargate Project.[^56] The Stargate Project materials donated by May span from 1972 to 1995.[^55]
